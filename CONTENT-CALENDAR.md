# Actifo Content Calendar — SEO/AEO Growth Plan

## Why this exists

The technical SEO/AEO foundation (structured data, sitemap, OG tags, FAQ schema) makes Actifo's site *eligible* to rank. It doesn't make it rank. "Social detox," "focused work," and "habit building" are competitive terms already owned by sites with years of content and backlinks. The only thing that actually closes that gap is publishing genuinely useful content on a consistent cadence, and getting it linked to from elsewhere.

This is a starting plan, not a locked schedule — swap topics as real user questions and support emails surface better ones.

## How this actually works (read before writing anything)

- **Cadence: 2 posts/month, sustainable beats sporadic.** A burst of 10 posts followed by six months of silence is worse for ranking than a steady 2/month. Don't commit to more than the team can actually keep up.
- **Every post links to something real.** Back to the FAQ, to a specific feature (QuickCompare, Mystery Box, Reward Store), or to the app download. Content that doesn't route somewhere is wasted effort.
- **Answer the actual question in the first 2–3 sentences.** This matters for both regular SEO (people bounce if they have to scroll) and AEO (AI answer engines pull the direct answer, not the preamble) — same instinct as how the FAQ section on the homepage is written.
- **Where these live:** a `/blog/` or `/resources/` section, one URL per post, each added to `sitemap.xml` as it ships. Not built yet — first post should come with that folder structure.
- **No fabricated stats or research.** If a post wants to cite a claim ("X% of people check their phone every Y minutes"), it needs a real, linkable source — or it doesn't get the claim.

## The three pillars

Every topic below maps back to one of these, since these are the actual terms being targeted:

| Pillar | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Social Detox** | Cutting back on specific apps (Instagram, YouTube), digital minimalism, what "detox" actually means day-to-day |
| **Focused Work** | Deep work, studying (PhysicsWallah audience), avoiding doomscrolling during work/study blocks |
| **Habit Building** | Streaks, gamification psychology, why rewards work, behavior change basics |

## Framework for months 4+ (repeatable)

Once the first 6 are live and you have real search/analytics data on what's landing, keep filling each pillar in rotation. Candidate topics to draw from as you go:

**Social Detox**
- Digital detox challenge ideas you can start today (solo or with friends)
- YouTube rabbit holes: how to stop autoplay from eating your evening
- What "doomscrolling" actually does, in plain terms (no fake stats — link real research if citing anything)

**Focused Work**
- Deep work for people who can't turn their phone off (job/family reasons)
- Study breaks that don't turn into 45 minutes of Instagram

**Habit Building**
- Why streaks work (and when they backfire)
- Mystery Box explained: the psychology of variable rewards, honestly
- QuickCompare: competing on focus with a friend, without oversharing your data

**Product spotlights (dual-purpose: SEO + onboarding)**
- Reward Store 101: turning coins into real vouchers
- Inside a QuickCompare battle, step by step

## Distribution (content alone doesn't rank — it needs to be found)

- Share each post to r/digitalminimalism, r/productivity, r/GetStudying (PhysicsWallah-adjacent) where genuinely relevant — not spammy self-promotion, actual participation.
- Submit to relevant app/tool directories (Product Hunt, AlternativeTo, G2) once there's enough content depth to point to.
- Cross-link every new post from the homepage FAQ or footer where it naturally fits, so it's not an orphan page.

## How to know it's working

- Search Console: impressions/clicks for "social detox," "focused work," "habit building," and long-tail variants, tracked monthly, not weekly (SEO moves slowly — don't panic-check).
- Which posts actually drive installs (UTM the Play Store links per post).
- Realistic timeline: expect months, not weeks, before competitive terms move. Long-tail posts (the "How to..." ones) typically show traction faster than the broad pillar posts.
